● Your Situation

  Campus Link solve real pain: Caraga State University instructors juggle 3 platforms (university website,
  Google Classroom, Messenger) to share information, and students still miss updates when absent. No single
  source of truth. No reliable notification. Firebase limitation on images/files forced you back to core
  strength — pure information delivery.

  That constraint is actually your advantage. You cannot compete on file sharing (Google Classroom owns that),
  so you compete on reliability, simplicity, and accountability — three things Messenger and Google Classroom
  are terrible at.

  Framework: Paul Graham's "Do Things That Don't Scale" + Pieter Levels' Ship Fast

  Two frameworks converge here:

  PG: Your first users (Caraga students + faculty) need manual recruitment, individual delight, and a "contained
   fire" strategy. Dominate one department, one batch of students, one concrete use case before expanding.

  Levels: Ship features fast, charge nothing (university context), iterate based on what users actually do. 70%
  completion is shipping. Features that do not get used — kill them.

  Recommendation: 5 Feature Directions That Beat Messenger

  1. Information FOMO Lock (High Impact, Low Build)

  Messenger fails because information drowns in group chat noise. Campus Link should guarantee no student misses
   critical info.

  Feature: "Missed While Absent" digest. When a student opens the app after being offline >4 hours, show a
  summary screen: "You missed: 2 new tasks from Prof. Santos, 1 announcement in CS-301 GC, 1 deadline changed."
  This single feature is more valuable than any Messenger feature for academic use.

  Why it wins: Messenger does not know what is "important." Campus Link does — because tasks and announcements
  are typed differently from casual chat.

  2. Accountability Layer (Your Create Task Is Already a Seed)

  Your Create Task lets faculty send title + description + links. Add:

  - Deadline acknowledgment: Student must tap "Acknowledge" (not just read). Faculty sees who has/has not
  acknowledged. No more "I did not know."
  - Auto-escalation: If a student has not acknowledged a task within 24 hours, push a notification. After 48
  hours, notify the faculty member. After 72 hours, auto-email the student's Carasu email as backup.
  - Completion proof (text-based): Since images/files are out, let students submit a text response or a link as
  proof. "Submit GitHub repo URL" or "Submit your answer in 500 characters."

  Why it wins: Messenger has zero task accountability. Google Classroom has it but buried. Yours is front and
  center.

  3. Unified Calendar Feed

  Every task, deadline, announcement, and class schedule lives in one scrollable timeline. Notifications push to
   the feed. The student NEVER checks Messenger for school info.

  Why it wins: Messenger is real-time chat. Campus Link is an academic information hub. Different jobs to be
  done.

  4. Confirmation Chain (The Viral Loop)

  When a faculty creates a task or announcement, the student must confirm receipt. That confirmation is visible
  to the class: "Juan confirmed Task 1." This creates social pressure to actually read and acknowledge. It also
  creates FOMO — students open the app because they see classmates confirming.

  Why it wins: Messenger cannot do this. Group admins cannot see who read what (unless they count reactions,
  which nobody does). Campus Link gives faculty actual visibility.

  5. "What Changed Since Last Time" Auto-Summary

  When a student opens the app after being away, auto-generated plain-text summary: "Since your last visit 3
  days ago: 5 new messages in CS-301, 2 tasks posted, 1 deadline extended to Friday."

  Why it wins: Messenger greets you with the last message, not the most important one. Campus Link surfaces
  priority first.

  Positioning: The Anti-Messenger

  Your marketing message to faculty: "Post it once in Campus Link. Students get it. They acknowledge it. You can
   see who knows and who does not. No chasing students in Messenger. No cross-posting to Google Classroom."

  To students: "Open Campus Link, see everything you missed. No scrolling through 200 Messenger messages. No
  'may nakalimutan ka bang assignment?' panic."

  Next Step

  Pick 1 feature from above. Build it this week. Test with 1 faculty member and their class (10-15 students). If
   they use it daily without reminders, you have found your wedge.

  I recommend starting with Deadline Acknowledgment (Feature #2) since your Create Task system is already built
  — it is the shortest path to a differentiated experience.
